A crooked star. A train heard from an empty platform. Choose a poem and see how much a few words can reveal about its speaker.

Try reading like a poet

Under the pier, the moon sets down
a silver plate on the water.

Notice. The two lines compare moonlight on the water to a silver plate. You can picture its round shape and gleaming surface.

Interpret. The words “sets down” make the moon sound gentle. A reader might imagine the water staying still long enough to hold the reflection. This reading connects a quoted detail to a particular effect.

Choose your evidence

Read all four lines aloud. Select up to two lines that you would bring to a discussion of the speaker’s feelings. You can change your selection.

Compare two readings

Both readers noticed something. Which reading explains more of the poem, from its opening to its last line? Pick one and test it against the lines you selected.

Which reading can I better support?

Write, then revise

Use the lines you selected to explain a change, a feeling or an idea in your own words. A different interpretation can work if you can show how the poem supports it.

Reread “The first mark”
My pencil shivers in the light.
The blank page waits for me to start.
One crooked star appears in sight.
Its five small lines unstick my heart.
